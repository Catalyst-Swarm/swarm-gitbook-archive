#### Avoiding plutocratic governance

[00:11](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=11) **Felix** -  The aim is to avoid plutocratic governance (rulership by those who hold the most tokens), and come to a more equal decision-making environment.\
[00:45](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=46) There have been 2 attempts to do this - [quadratic voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_voting) was implemented early in DeepFunding, and research has been done on it; and later, a [contribution score](https://deepfunding.ai/proposal/community-contribution-scores/) was developed by [Photrek](https://www.photrek.io/home).

#### What is a contribution score?

Contribution scores assume that the more someone participates, the more they build up knowledge and experience, and that this helps them make higher-quality decisions.

[1:44](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=104) Photrek's contribution score focuses on contributions such as submitting proposals, commenting on proposals, etc, on [Swae](https://proposals.deepfunding.ai/home) (the old DeepFunding platform); and also the age of your wallet. These feed into an algorithm that is used to weight people's voting power.\
[2:30 ](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=150)To further develop this work, a group started in mid-2023 with [New Mindflow](https://www.newmindflow.com/) (Anamaria and Rares) and Photrek. It led to a [successful DeepFunding Round 3 proposal](https://proposals.deepfunding.ai/graduated/accepted/c2af78f2-b4fa-4e73-9859-8f806006f580) called "_Requirements for a Deep Funding Contribution Token_".

#### Is AI less forgiving than we are? Monitoring, forgiving and assessing each other

[13:41](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=821) **Fly** - In a study where AI models examined criminals' data and suggested an outcome, the AI was generally less forgiving than its human counterparts. I imagine AI could be useful in spotting outliers in the data.\
[14:42](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=882) **Curtis** - AI shouldn't make _**decisions**_ - but if you give it access to all the metrics, you can query it about someone else, to learn about them and make your own decision about them. \
[16:39](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=999) **Kenric** - Research on the [Prisoner's Dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner's_dilemma) (the foundation for [game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory)) shows that a) agents develop a reputation over time as to whether they are co-operative and b) agents are forgiving if someone wrongs them, which builds a collaborative community. It's important to select for group behaviour, not individual behaviour; and avoid our good intentions around reputation turning into [surveillance capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance_capitalism). Part of beneficial AI is to build things in a non-exploitative way. If we're monitors of each other, how do we stop that becoming perverse? Maybe one way is to build reputation models about clusters of social networks, not about individuals.\
[19:14](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1154) **Felix -** Those who game a system are putting their individual interest above that of the group. Our systems often unwittingly incentivise that.\
[19:42](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1182) **Kenric** - If you build reputation on social networks, then there's peer pressure to maintain good social networks and not behave selfishly.

#### Data about social networks, not individuals - and the right to opt out

[20:15](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1215) **Felix** - Contribution tokens create an ever-growing library of data about verified individual contributions in an ecosystem. This can be difficult to manage. Having an LLM reading through contributions, and categorising or identifying social networks, means we wouldn't have to rely on humans to map this data. Once networks are identified, then we can incentivise them.\
[21:49](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1309) **Kenric** - The networks are not too hard to define - we have a public ledger, so it's just the transactions between wallets.\
[22:24](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1344) **Tommy** - It reminds me of the "black box" of 1980s credit scores - you couldn't opt out, nobody knew how it was assessing them, it penalised people for using cash, and helped governments track people. With a contribution token as a way of increasing voting power - how do you opt out, other than by not voting?

#### Reputation risk, public transactions - and have we even implemented quadratic voting at all?

[24:05](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1445) **Kenric** - When we do private transactions, we are each responsible for the risks; but here, we are trying to manage a _public_ treasury and _public_ decisions. There is an incentive to exploit the treasury, hence there's a reputation risk. I _**thought**_ we had implemented quadratic voting in SingularityNET - but then I re-read Glen Weyl's [papers](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/pandp.20181002), and realised we haven't. Quadratic voting, as he proposed it, is not about weighting wealth - it's about weighting the strength of your conviction about a public choice. But there's another step that no-one's ever done - the original design cut through the problem of reputational risk because it wasn't a snaphot, like we do in crypto - it was an actual payment to the community, so that you bear the cost of acting poorly, because you have to pay the community for your votes. A person has to calculate "How much is this project worth to me?" and they won't want to pay more than that for it. It's a big constraint, and different from how we have been thinking about voting.

#### Accounting for what people learn from their "mistakes"?

[28:12](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1692) **Fly** - Wallet reputation, in DeFi terms, doesn't necessarily account for the wallet owner's learnings. In governance, if someone votes in an extreme way, there are no repercussions. How could a wallet-owner's learnings be accessed, aggregated, or used?\
[29:58](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1799) **Felix** - How does someone prove they have learnt about something? I like [Andamio](https://www.andamio.io/)'s approach - you prove learning by showing that you can _make use of_ your knowledge; there are specific actions that you must do to demonstrate that you have learnt. For example, if someone gets funded in DeepFunding, this must mean they know how to write a proposal.\
[32:11](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=1931) **Newman** - So we are talking about assessment. You can do that by verbal knowledge - ask them a question, ask them to write an essay - or you can measure it by action. And the learner gains a lot of new experiences by going through the process of doing the action. We do measure contribution through studen t learning targets. As Curtis said, an AI assessor can have many more data points.\
[34:47](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2087) **Fly** - While extreme voting is bad in most cases, there is nuance that can be enabled with AI.

#### We all interpret the data differently

[35:38](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2138) **Tevo** - Everyone has their own way of interpreting the metrics, to build up an image of a person. In our open-source community, we naturally look for people working on similar things to ourselves. And we want to measure what people _**say**_ they'll do, vs whether they do it.

#### Minority views, centralisation, and a "plutocracy" of reputation?

[38:00](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2280) **Ubio** - Is it possible to have a "plutocracy" of reputation? Plutocracy refers to those who have a lot of wealth - but could those with a high reputation have a similarly unfair influence, and how is your system balancing this?\
[38:44](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2324) **Kenric** - That's a legitimate concern. The articles by Eric Posner and Glen Weyl (for example [Quadratic Voting as Efficient Corporate Governance](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1642\&context=law_and_economics), 2013; [Voting Squared: Quadratic Voting in Democratic Politics](https://scholarship.law.vanderbilt.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1176\&context=vlr), 2015. See also their book [_Radical Markets: Uprooting Capitalism and Democracy for a Just Society_](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvc77c4f)_,_ 2018) review the weaknesses in democracy itself. Crypto uses "1 coin 1 vote" and we know it leads to centralisation - but they point out that even "1 person 1 vote", however you weight it, tends to converge to a majority faction that rules, and a minority faction that gets abused. So weighting it with reputation will change the composition of the majority, but won't build a plural system. It's about what mechanisms a minority has to express their disagreement with a majority.\
[42:23](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2543) **Felix** - If you have 100 people, and just 10 of them align, they will dominate. We tested this out, for a joke, in the early days of Catalyst.

#### Contextual reputation, our "nearest neighbours" - and what do we want to incentivise?

[43:22](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2602) **Roberto** - Voting systems in society are supposed to direct society to an outcome - so what is the philosophy behind the system, what does it want to incentivise? Different types of expertise or reputation will be important for different decisions. Our identities, our reputations, are very layered and contextual - familial, professional, social - and the API needs to understand all those nuanced parts of your behaviour. See Dr Mihaela Ulieru's papers on [emergent engineering](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228348641_Emergent_engineering_A_radical_paradigm_shift) - she compares regulating a complex system to how a flock of starlings moves, without needing a central rule or hierarchy, just by communicating with their immediate neighbours. So a reputation system can focus on your relation with your nearest neighbour. The only people who know you, are those you interact with.\
[49:33](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=2973) **Curtis** - I take inspiration from how Gimbalabs is deploying their credentials system. They compile the metrics - "here's the courses you took, the projects you worked on" - but they do **not** say "this means you're worth this or that". It's up to the individual organisation or person to determine how they value it.\
[50:19](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=3019) **Kenric** - [Ben Goertzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Goertzel) said something similar - you collect many inputs that go into a computational engine, but then you have to be able to make different inferences, depending on the choice you are trying to make. And the purpose of a reputation system is to make a probability prediction about performance on something. So the context is everything - your likely performance will depend on what you're trying to do. It's not about one metric, but many.

#### The problem of pseudo-activity

[58:11](https://youtu.be/Bn-tXIJ0ZpE?t=3490) **Fly** - To quote Slavoj Žižek - the problem is not inactivity or passivity, but pseudo-activity. ## TL:DNR

* Reputation is contextual. Individuals each have different ideas about the value of a contribution - it depends what you are looking for! Organisations or groups also have their own collective idea, often based on what the group's ethos is, and/or what it needs and what it wants to incentivise.
* So - will a contribution token address individual perceptions of a contribution's value, or group perceptions - or both? Or can we remove the whole issue of "value" from a token, and simply use it to collect data that each of us can assess according to our own ideas of value? And perhaps to recognise patterns of behaviour in the data?
* Is "assessing contribution" the same as "judging"? Should we assess what is good about someone's contribution; what is bad; or both? Or should we avoid "good/bad" judgements about people's actions, and look instead at what someone has learnt?
* Should AI only be used to organise the data, so that humans can assess it and decide what they value - or should it also be used to make judgements? Is it possible for AI to assess contribution "neutrally" or "objectively", given that a human is prompt-engineering the AI, and will have their own individual perceptions?

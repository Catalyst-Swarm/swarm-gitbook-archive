Most DAOs work on the basis of plutocratic voting systems where the more assets you hold the more power you have. ### Privacy concerns

**Jan** **Horlings** - Privacy concerns - [14:05](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=845)

I am always a bit concerned about privacy. Contribution scores are very important for many reasons some may be good and some may be bad. We may often think from a perspective of trust in our own governments and our freedom. But if you are in a country that is not friendly either in the present or future then how comfortable would you be that all your activity would be on some blockchain ? The abstraction of a wallet address may not be sufficient assurance of privacy.

"The General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, abbreviated GDPR) is a European Union regulation on information privacy in the European Union (EU) and the European Economic Area (EEA)."
{% endhint %}

Also on the basis of [GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu) (General Data Protection Regulation), that is the right to be forgotten, it would be preferable to be in control of my own data. The removal of data is a challenge on a blockchain.

**Jan** **Horlings** - Neutral on tokenization - [15:50](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=950)

I am not in favour or against tokenizing. I am in favour of some kind of collection of contribution scores. The more sources we have for contribution the better. A single source is easier to game and is not so realistic as a range of sources.

**Nicola - Storm Partners** - possible solutions - [16:39](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=999)

{% hint style="info" %}
"A zero-knowledge proof \[...] is a method by which one party (the prover) can prove to another party (the verifier) that a given statement is true, while avoiding conveying to the verifier any information _beyond_ the mere fact of the statement's truth."

I think there are some solutions available that would enable us to protect privacy and avoid plutocratic hegemony. I refer to [zero knowledge solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_proof) particularly in regard to identity. ### The legal perspective&#x20;

**Nicola - Storm Partners** - The legal perspective - [30:59](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=1859)

From a legal point of view the question of "Who owns the data ?" should be answered in the context of all the activities of Deep Funding. This is why we need a legal entity that identifies who runs Deep Funding.

The main point is how the governance of this legal enity works. So all the questions posed in relation to data depends upon what is approved by the legal entity ("the DAO").

How this is implemented is another question, because a lot of these decisions are self-implemented. Either we decision a smart contract with a kill switch that can delete data or there needs some delegated persons (responsible custodians). With most DAOs we work with we opt for a delegated person.

As much as we would like to have a fully decentralised, automated organisation in the end a human hand is still necessary.

It is important that people who have delegated responsibilities are overseen and legally bound to decisions taken by the DAO.

**Jan Horlings** - The DAO and data ownership - [33:16](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=1996)

If the DAO can decide what is done with the data then it is owed by the DAO. Which means it is not mine. So I may have a problem if I want to delete my data and the DAO says no.

**Nicola - Storm Partners** - That depends on the terms and conditions provided by the DAO in respect of data. From the beginning you can establish the right to delete your data. These terms would be drafted at the same time as the governance framework.

**Jan Horlings** - We may want a seperate DAO to be in charge of reputation.

**Jan Horlings** - At a high level you would still have a reputation score and that represents some value.

Additionally if you abstract everything away you will lose future use case possibilities.&#x20;

One reason to have a knowledge graph is to have one set of data which can be used in many different ways. If we can store contribution or social interactions in some level of metadata in a knowlege graph this would provide flexible use cases.

If you abstract all this away into a number you lose a lot of the power of this data.

**Rafael Presa** - It be would good to have granularity of data - [40:00](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=2400)

**Felix Weber** - It is important to consider what tools we would use - [42:18](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=2538)

So we need to consider what is the relation between the tool that is used and the individual user. How much the tool abstracts data and how much data the user keeps to themselves.

A focus on the quality not the quantity of data is needed. With a clear structure.

**Nicola - Storm Partners** - We are based in Switzerland - [45:10](https://youtu.be/Jk12DJb5Yao?t=2710)

We are based in Switzerland but we operate in many different jurisdictions. We have contributed to writing the Decentralised Autonomous Organisation Act of the Marshall Islands. From our (legal) perspective every DAO should be incorporated. A court of law would define it as a single partnership with a limited liability.

I would never issue a token without a legal entity. I would establish a governance framework in parallel to the legal structure.

They have worked with lawyers from the beginning about how to translate the struture the community is discussing to something that is legally implementable. To avoid lengthly community discussion on solutions which are not legally feasible.
